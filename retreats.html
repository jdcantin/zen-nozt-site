Sesshin

Intensive Zen Retreats

Sesshin (literally “touching the mind”) are intensive Zen retreats lasting from one to several days. During sesshin, practitioners follow a rigorous schedule of zazen, kinhin (walking meditation), meals taken in the traditional ōryōki style, and work practice (samu).

Sesshin provides an opportunity to deepen one's practice beyond the regular weekly schedule. The extended periods of sitting, the silence, and the shared rhythm of the sangha create conditions that are difficult to replicate in daily life.

What to Expect

  • Noble silence is maintained throughout, except during dharma talks and brief necessary communication.
  • Multiple zazen periods each day, typically 30–40 minutes each, with kinhin between periods.
  • Vegetarian meals are prepared and eaten in the traditional Zen manner.
  • Work practice (samu) — cleaning, cooking, and maintaining the dojo are part of the practice.

Requirements

Participants should have some experience with zazen before attending sesshin. Temple membership is required. Contact us if you are new and wish to participate — we can help you prepare.
