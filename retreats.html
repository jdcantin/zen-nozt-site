Sesshin

Zen Retreats

Sesshin (literally “touching the mind”) are extended Zen retreats lasting from one to several days. During sesshin, practitioners follow a schedule of zazen, kinhin (walking meditation), meals, and work practice (samu).

Sesshin provides an opportunity to deepen one's practice beyond the regular weekly schedule. The extended periods of sitting, the silence, and the shared rhythm of the sangha create conditions that are difficult to replicate in daily life.

What to Expect

  • Silence is generally maintained throughout, except during dharma talks and brief necessary communication.
  • Multiple zazen periods each day, typically 30–40 minutes each, with kinhin between periods.
  • Vegetarian meals are eaten together in community with the sangha.
  • Work practice (samu) — physical or mental work performed for the benefit of the sangha in a spirit of wholehearted giving and concentrated effort.

Requirements

Sesshin is open to anyone. Participants normally have some experience with daily zazen practice before attending sesshin. Temple membership is not required. Contact us if you are new and wish to participate — we can help you prepare.
