Before the Lineage

Classic Texts

Zazen is older than any one teacher. These are the foundational texts of the Soto Zen tradition — some over a thousand years old — that Kodo Sawaki, Taisen Deshimaru and Robert Livingston each studied, chanted, and taught from in turn. They are gathered here rather than folded into any one teacher’s page because they belong to the whole lineage.

Eihei Dogen Zenji (1200–1253)

Founder of the Soto school in Japan. His writings — collected in the Shobogenzo — are the philosophical bedrock beneath every teisho in this collection.

Fukanzazengi

The Way is fundamentally perfect. It penetrates everything. How could it depend upon practice and realization? The vehicle of the Dharma is free and unobstructed. Why is concentrated effort necessary? The truth is that the Great Body is well beyond the dust of this world. Who could believe there is a way to dust it?

Sansui-Kyo

1 Mountains and waters right now are the actualization of the ancient Buddha way. Each, abiding in its phenomenal expression, realizes completeness. Because mountains and waters have been active since before the Empty Eon, they are alive at this moment. Because they have been the self since before form arose they are emancipation realization.

Uji

1 An ancient buddha said: For the time being stand on top of the highest peak. For the time being proceed along the bottom of the deepest ocean. For the time being three heads and eight arms. For the time being an eight- or sixteen-foot body. For the time being a staff or whisk. For the time being a pillar or lantern. For the time being the sons of Zhang and Li. For the time being the earth and sky.

Zazen Shin

The essential point of the many Buddhas and Patriarchs is actualized by non-thinking and realized by non-opposition. As it is actualized by non-thinking, this actualization is unconsciously and naturally intimate. As it is realized by non-opposition, this accomplishment is an unconscious and natural certification.

Hannya Shingyo — The Heart Sutra

Chanted daily in Zen temples worldwide. This short sutra distills the Prajnaparamita teachings on emptiness (ku) into a single page.

Hannya Shingyo

The Great Sutra of Profound Essential Wisdom and Beyond The Bodhisattva of true compassion, Through deep practice in Great Wisdom, Understands that the body and the five skandas are only emptiness. And with this understanding, he helps all who suffer. Oh Sariputra, Phenomena is no different than ku, ku no different from phenomena. Phenomena becomes ku, ku becomes phenomena. The five skandas too are only phenomena.

Hui Neng (638–713) — The Platform Sutra

The Sixth Patriarch of Chan Buddhism in China. His autobiographical teaching, the Treasure of Law Sutra, is one of the few sutras attributed to a native-born Chinese teacher rather than translated from Sanskrit.

Treasure of Law

Treasure of Law Sutra (Platform Sutra of the Sixth Patriarch) by Hui Neng Once, when the Patriarch had arrived at Pao Lin Monastery, Prefect Wei of Shao Chou and other officials went there to ask him to deliver public lectures on Buddhism in the hall of Ta Fan Temple in the City of Canton.

Sekito Kisen (700–790)

An early Chan master whose short poem on a life of simple, non-grasping practice remains one of the most quoted verses in Zen teaching.

Strawroof Hut

I have built a straw-roof hut where nothing is of value. After eating, I relax and take a nap. When the hut was finished, shoots appeared. Now weeds cover everything. The man in the hut lives peacefully, without ties inside or outside. He doesn't want to live where the ordinary live.
