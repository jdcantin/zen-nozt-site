The Lineage Speaks

Teachings

Zazen is not a philosophy to study — it is a practice to sit. But the teachers of our lineage left words, too: talks given after zazen, notebooks kept for decades, poems written in a single sitting. Gathered here from the Temple’s own archive, these are not blog posts or commentary. They are the voices of the people who carried this practice from Japan to Paris to New Orleans.

The Masters
Who Kodo Sawaki, Taisen Deshimaru, and Robert Livingston were, and how the Transmission passed from one to the next.
Kodo Sawaki Roshi
The wandering monk who refused every temple offered him. Teishos on the kesa, discrimination, and true religion.
Taisen Deshimaru Roshi
The disciple who brought Zen to the West. Kusen on zazen, satori, and the Ten New Principles of Zen.
Robert Livingston Roshi
Founder of the American Zen Association and this Temple. Kusen and mondo on practice, strong mind, and true Zen.
Classic Texts
The foundational sutras and poems that came before all three — Dogen, Hui Neng, Sekito, and the Heart Sutra.

A note on this collection

These pages are drawn from the Temple’s own teaching archive, first published in the early 2000s and long offline. We have re-set them in the Temple’s current design rather than reproduce the old formatting, and organized them by teacher rather than as one long page. Some pieces are excerpted here; the emphasis in each case is on giving a real, substantial taste of the teaching rather than a summary.
