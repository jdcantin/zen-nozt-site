Glossary

Key terms from the Soto Zen tradition, particularly as used in the Sawaki–Deshimaru–Livingston lineage.

Bodhidharma
Indian monk who brought Zen Buddhism from India to China in the 5th or 6th century.
Bodhisattva
One who seeks enlightenment for the benefit of all beings.
Buddha
The 'Awakened One.' Historically, Siddhartha Gautama (c. 5th century BCE). In Zen, Buddha-nature is inherent in all beings.
Dana
Generosity; the practice of giving. One of the six paramitas.
Deshimaru, Taisen
Japanese Soto Zen master (1914–1982) who brought Zen to Europe. Student of Kodo Sawaki.
Dharma
The teachings of the Buddha; also, the true nature of reality.
Dōgen, Eihei
Japanese Zen master (1200–1253), founder of the Soto school in Japan. Author of the Shōbōgenzō.
Dojo
Place of practice. The room where zazen is practiced.
Gasshō
Palms pressed together at chest height as a gesture of respect and greeting.
Gyoji
Continuous practice; the unbroken practice that sustains the Way.
Hara
The lower abdomen; center of breathing and energy in zazen.
Hishiryo
Beyond thinking; the consciousness during zazen that is neither thinking nor not-thinking.
Hokkaijōin
The cosmic mudra — the hand position during zazen, left hand on right, thumb tips touching.
Kekkafuza
Full lotus posture.
Kesa
The traditional robe of a Buddhist monk, symbolizing the transmission of dharma.
Kinhin
Walking meditation practiced between periods of zazen.
Koan
A paradoxical story or question used in Rinzai Zen as a meditation focus. Less emphasized in Soto.
Kyōsaku
The 'encouragement stick' used during zazen to help maintain alertness (by request only).
Livingston, Robert
American Soto Zen master (1933–2021), founder of the American Zen Association and of Muhozan Kōzenji (the New Orleans Zen Temple). Student of Taisen Deshimaru.
Mondo
Dharma question-and-answer exchange between teacher and student.
Mushotoku
Without seeking profit; the fundamental attitude of zazen — practice without goal or expectation.
Ōryōki
The formal style of eating meals during sesshin, using nested bowls in a ritualized manner.
Paramita
A 'perfection' or transcendent practice. The six paramitas are: giving, ethics, patience, effort, meditation, and wisdom.
Rakusu
A small sewn garment worn around the neck, representing the kesa. Given at ordination.
Samu
Work practice — cleaning, cooking, and other manual work done as meditation.
Sangha
The community of practitioners. One of the Three Treasures (Buddha, Dharma, Sangha).
Sawaki, Kodo
Japanese Soto Zen master (1880–1965) known for reviving shikantaza practice. Teacher of Taisen Deshimaru.
Sesshin
'Touching the mind.' An intensive Zen retreat, typically 1–7 days of rigorous zazen practice.
Shikantaza
'Just sitting.' The core practice of Soto Zen — sitting without object, goal, or gaining idea.
Shōbōgenzō
Master Dōgen's masterwork, 'Treasury of the True Dharma Eye.'
Sutra
A Buddhist scripture or discourse, often chanted during ceremonies.
Zabuton
The rectangular mat placed beneath the zafu for cushioning the knees during zazen.
Zafu
The round meditation cushion used for sitting zazen.
Zazen
Seated meditation — the fundamental practice of Zen Buddhism.
